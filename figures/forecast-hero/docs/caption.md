# Caption — forecast-hero

**A base model predicts from the dominant features; a second model predicts a relative correction from the non-dominant features and the base forecast.** Features are ranked by importance and split at index p into a dominant group 𝐬⁽¹⁾ = (x₁, …, x_p) and a non-dominant group 𝐬⁽²⁾ = (x_{p+1}, …, x_d); a domain-driven split works as well. The base model outputs the base forecast ŷ₁ = f₁(𝐬⁽¹⁾) and is trained on the observed value y. The second-layer model outputs the predicted correction α̂ = f₂([𝐬⁽²⁾; ŷ₁]) from the concatenation of the non-dominant features and the base forecast, and the corrected forecast is ŷ = (1 + α̂) ŷ₁. Both models are LightGBM tree ensembles; the base model is drawn as the larger one. Solid arrows are the prediction flow, used in training and at inference alike (training predictions ŷ₁ come from the same f₁). Dashed arrows and the dashed box are training only: the cost-optimization block takes ŷ₁ and the observed y and computes the correction target α⋆ = arg min over a ∈ [−β, β] of ℓ(y, (1 + a) ŷ₁), the second model's target. Here β bounds the target search and ℓ is the forecast loss; α⋆ is computed from y and ŷ₁, it is not an observed quantity. Hats mark predicted quantities, the star the optimised target. At inference no target is computed: only the two trained models and the combination run. Bottom: the corrected forecast (orange) follows events that the base forecast (dashed blue) misses, though not perfectly. The curves are an illustrative toy signal, not results from the paper. Future observations are shown for comparison and are not supplied to the models at inference. The History | Forecast line marks the forecast origin.

Detailed version (`forecast-hero-detailed`): the same caption; the figure also shows the search bound a ∈ [−β, β] under the arg min and shades the base-forecast error (grey) between y and ŷ₁.
